## 1. Cherry-picked / wide-real-time-span temporal window (`dtime`) — tested on FROG, negative result

**Status:** implemented (data-loading *and* training level) and properly tested via a real retrain, not just a zero-shot smoke test. **Result: regression, not improvement, on FROG specifically** — see `docs/RESEARCH.md` Section 8.6.2 for the full writeup. Keeping this entry for the DROW follow-up, which hasn't been tried.

**Motivation:** FROG runs at 40 Hz, but the T=5 window was originally sized ("half a gait cycle") assuming DROW's ~10 Hz rate — so on FROG, T=5 consecutive frames span only ~100–125 ms of real time, not the ~500 ms the design rationale assumes. A person barely displaces beam-to-beam over 125 ms, which may be a big part of why the temporal architectures aren't clearly beating single-frame LFE-Peaks on FROG.

**What's implemented:** `DROW_Dataset.get_scan()` / `FROG_Dataset.get_scan()` gained a `dtime` stride parameter (`library/follow_the_drow/datasets/drow_dataset.py`, `frog_dataset.py`) — `get_scan(seq, scan_id, time_window, dtime=N)` spreads the same `time_window` frames over `N`x more real time. Backward compatible: `dtime=1` reproduces the original contiguous-window behaviour exactly (verified). Both the eval path (`evaluate.py --eval-dtime N`, for zero-shot testing of existing checkpoints) and the *training* path (`train.py --time-frame T --dtime N`, threaded through `LidarFrameDataset`/`_setup_datasets`) support it.

**What was tried:** FullScanTCN retrained from scratch on FROG with `--time-frame 10 --dtime 20` (10 frames, 500 ms apart, spanning ~4.5 s) — same hyperparameters otherwise as the existing `fullscan_tcn.best.pth` (69.5–69.7% wp-AUC baseline). Result: **54.8%** (val-set) — a ~15-point regression, not an improvement. FullScanTCN was chosen over SpaceTimeCNN because its cheap per-beam-TCN temporal collapse makes widening `T` inexpensive, and because it (unlike SpaceTimeCNN) had an unsolved FP problem worth a real fix.

**Why it regressed, most likely:** FROG has no odometry, so every temporal window is raw unaligned frame-stacking. A short window bounds how much the robot's own uncompensated motion can corrupt beam correspondence between frames; a ~4.5 s window gives that same ego-motion 36× longer to accumulate, actively fighting against the "clearer person-motion signal" the wider window was meant to provide. The `SimpleTracker` invocation-rate sweep (Section 8.6.1) found the same pattern independently (wider real-time gaps between tracker invocations lost to the plain baseline) — two separate mechanisms pointing at the same structural cause, not a coincidence.

**Revised next step:** this specific idea is not worth pursuing further *on FROG* — the missing-odometry confound looks structural, not a tuning problem. It's still worth testing on **DROW**, which has real odometry (item 3 below) — that would isolate the "clearer motion signal" effect from the ego-motion corruption that dominated here, since a DROW-native (or DROW-adapted) tracker/window could actually compensate for the sensor's own motion instead of assuming it away.

**Update — revised again:** the T=10/dtime=20 result above was the wrong data point to generalize from. A proper T=5 sweep over dtime ∈ {0, 1, 5, 10} on FROG (SpaceTimeCNN, full retrain per point, evaluated on **both** train and test folds to rule out overfitting) found:

| dtime | window span | FROG train wp-AUC | FROG test wp-AUC |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (no temporal diversity) | none | 77.1% | 77.1% |
| 1 (original default) | ~125ms | 76.7% | 76.7% |
| **5** | **~625ms** | **80.7%** | **80.7%** |
| 10 | ~1.25s | 78.1% | 78.0% |

Train ≈ test at every point (no overfitting — expected given FROG's ~108k-frame train split). dtime=5 is a real, non-overfitting +4pp peak over the dtime=0/1 baseline; dtime=10 partially reverts but stays above baseline, consistent with — not contradicting — the T=10/dtime=20 collapse: there's a real sweet spot around dtime=5, and the ego-motion-corruption cost (nothing compensates for it on FROG) overtakes the wider-window benefit somewhere between dtime=10 and dtime=20, not immediately past dtime=1. **Conclusion revised: temporal fusion does help on FROG — the original dtime=1 (~125ms) window was simply too narrow to capture real motion, not that temporal information is structurally useless there.** This also bears on the original "why doesn't our temporal architecture beat single-frame LFE-Peaks on FROG" question that motivated this whole line of investigation — worth re-checking that comparison at dtime=5 specifically.

## 2. LFE-PPN's remaining DROW gap — two real bugs found and fixed, one still open

**Status:** partially resolved, parked for later — not urgent enough to block the DROW pivot below.

`LFEPeaksDetector`/`LFEPPNDetector` zero-padded shorter scans (DROW's 450 beams) into the first N of 720 trained slots, leaving the rest as padding — wrong on two independent counts, both found by direct code inspection and fixed in `library/follow_the_drow/detectors/lfe_detector.py`:

1. **Padding value inverted**: padded with raw range 0.0, which normalizes to ~0.98 ("very close obstacle") instead of the documented "missing data → 0.0" convention (needs a *far* raw value, e.g. `_SCAN_FAR`). Fixed, but alone barely moved DROW's numbers (LFE-Peaks 24.0%→24.4%, LFE-PPN 11.7%→10.7% — actually slightly worse) — not the dominant bug.
2. **Beam-index-as-angle mismatch**: DROW's 450 beams span ±112.25° at ~0.5°/beam (wider FoV, coarser resolution) vs. FROG's 720 beams at ±90°/~0.25°/beam (trained convention) — copying beam *index* i into slot i silently treated it as if it meant the same real-world angle in both datasets. Fixed by replacing padding with proper angular resampling (`_resample_to_trained()`: `np.interp` onto FROG's real ±90°/720-beam grid — DROW's FoV fully contains FROG's, so no padding is needed at all, every target angle has a real DROW measurement). This was the dominant bug: LFE-Peaks 24.4%→**34.5%**, LFE-PPN 10.7%→**24.0%**.

**Still open:** LFE-PPN's 24.0% remains right at the "maybe still broken" threshold that started this investigation, and improved by a smaller absolute margin than LFE-Peaks got from the identical input-side fix — despite both sharing the same fixed preprocessing, only the output head differs. LFE-PPN already had two *other* unrelated bugs found earlier this session (hardcoded anchor count, broken recall metric) — enough of a track record to suspect a third rather than assume the residual gap is just cross-dataset transfer penalty. Not investigated further; revisit if LFE-PPN's DROW number matters for a later comparison.

## 3. Real ego-motion-compensated tracking (for datasets *with* odometry)

**Status:** not started.

FROG ships no odometry at all (confirmed: its `.h5` files have no pose/odom field) — the temporal-consensus tracker being built now (Section 8's consensus subsection) explicitly assumes a static sensor on FROG as a result, per team decision, since there's nothing to be done about missing data. DROW *does* carry real per-frame odometry (`.odom2` files, verified non-zero). If/when the tracker is extended to DROW, it should ego-motion-compensate the track cache (subtract the robot's own transform between frames before matching predictions to new detections) rather than assuming a static frame — this is expected to matter much more there than it would on a truly static sensor, and DROW is the fairer testbed for validating the tracking mechanism's core assumptions independent of the missing-odometry confound.

**Update:** done, for DROW. `SimpleTracker` (`library/follow_the_drow/utils/tracking.py`) now ego-motion-compensates both rotation and translation, and `aligned_raw_scan()` applies the same correction to training input. Confirmed to matter: fixing a rotation-sign bug found in `aligned_raw_scan()` took the DROW-native SpaceTimeCNN baseline from 15.0%→18.5% wp-AUC on test. FROG itself is still untouched (no odometry to compensate with) — see item 4.

## 4. Infer pseudo-odometry for FROG from static-landmark scan matching

**Status:** next up — chosen priority (team decision, overriding the initially-recommended DROW-first order). Rationale: the item-1 dtime sweep found FROG dtime=5 at 80.7% wp-AUC, the only point where our own architecture is in beat-SOTA territory at all, and since our full-scan design can't compete with LFE-Peaks/LFE-PPN on speed (per-beam-cutout vs. single-scan input, a structural gap, not a tuning one), pushing that one accuracy lead further is currently the most exciting lever available. **Verified:** re-ran LFE-Peaks on the same full, non-subsampled FROG test split (120,396 frames) used for the dtime sweep — the only LFE-Peaks FROG numbers on file before this were both run with `--eval-stride 40` (3,010 frames), not apples-to-apples. Clean result: **LFE-Peaks 74.8% wp-AUC vs. our SpaceTimeCNN dtime=5 80.7%** — a genuine +5.9pp lead, confirmed under matched methodology.

**Motivation:** FROG's zero-history ablation showed temporal input barely helps single-frame LFE-Peaks, and item 1's wide-stride retrain actively regressed — both consistent with the same root cause: FROG has no odometry, so every temporal window `aligned_raw_scan()` builds for it is raw, uncompensated frame-stacking (rotation/translation correction are no-ops there by construction). This session just confirmed, on DROW, that *correct* ego-motion compensation gives a real if partial improvement (15.0%→18.5% wp-AUC, item 3's update above) — the natural question is whether FROG's much larger training set could show an even cleaner win if it had odometry to correct with too.

**Proposed approach:** a small algorithmic static-landmark detector on FROG's raw scans (corner/edge extraction via local range-curvature discontinuities — standard 2D LiDAR-odometry preprocessing), then a robust frame-to-frame rigid-transform estimate (RANSAC or ICP-with-outlier-rejection) fit to matched static points across consecutive scans. Feed the resulting per-frame pseudo-odometry into the same `aligned_raw_scan()` / `SimpleTracker` pipeline already built for DROW.

**Why it's more tractable than full LiDAR odometry:** only short-horizon relative deltas are needed (T=5, a few hundred ms) — no globally-consistent trajectory, no loop closure, so drift accumulation isn't a concern the way it would be for mapping. A robust registration step is also naturally tolerant of the minority of scan points that belong to moving people rather than static structure (FROG's whole point), so the "static" filtering doesn't need to be perfect upfront.

**Key risk:** this session's own `aligned_raw_scan()` bug is a cautionary tale — a wrong-sign or noisy correction measurably *worsened* the training signal relative to no correction at all. A scan-matching estimator built for this would need the same hand-derived-ground-truth verification (`tests/test_geometry.py`-style) before being trusted in a real retrain, not just "looks plausible" from a visual check.

**Relationship to item 1:** if this works, it would let the wide-stride/`dtime` experiment be legitimately retried on FROG, rather than permanently parked as DROW-only.

**Update — implemented and generated.** Built `library/follow_the_drow/utils/odometry_estimation.py`: trimmed point-to-point ICP (Kabsch/SVD closed-form rigid-transform solve, iterative nearest-neighbour correspondence with distance-based outlier rejection) between consecutive raw scans, dead-reckon-integrated into a per-frame (x, y, theta) trajectory. Ended up simpler than the originally-proposed corner/edge feature extraction: dense trimmed ICP is a more standard, equally-robust technique that avoids a separate hand-tuned feature-detection step. Verified with 10 unit tests (`tests/test_odometry_estimation.py`) including end-to-end synthetic trajectories (forward/rotation/combined/sideways/static motion, each checked against known ground truth) before running on real data at all — the key-risk cautionary tale above was taken seriously.

Generated for the full 120,396-frame FROG recording (`generate_frog_odometry.py`, ~4.4ms/frame, ~9min total) into `frog_11-36_12-43_train_val_odom.npz`, which `FROG_Dataset._load_or_fake_odom()` already picks up automatically (no dataset-loading code changes needed) — covers train/val/test alike, since FROG's "test" split is the same one recording read unfiltered, not a separate file.

One real issue found and fixed during generation: a first pass produced a single 1.1m single-frame jump and, more importantly, a *sustained* cluster of large jumps (frames ~81655-81822) traced to a large open-space region where ~97% of beams exceeded the valid range, leaving too few points for reliable registration — a classic scan-matching failure mode, not a code bug. Fixed with a physically-motivated per-step sanity clamp in `integrate_trajectory()` (reject any single-frame delta implying an implausible velocity, fall back to zero motion for that one frame) plus a higher `min_inliers`. After the fix, max single-step distance dropped from 1.10m to 0.20m (the clamp bound).

The regenerated full trajectory still shows large *long-horizon* drift (~600 degrees of net heading rotation over the ~76-minute recording, and a few hundred metres of apparent net displacement) — expected for pure dead-reckoning with no loop closure over that time span, not itself a red flag. What actually matters was checked directly: `aligned_raw_scan()` and `SimpleTracker` only ever consume *short-horizon* deltas (within one T-frame window, or between consecutive annotated frames), never the absolute trajectory, and those short-horizon deltas (checked at dtime=1/5/10/20 across the *entire* dataset, not just a sample) stay small, bounded, and physically plausible throughout, scaling sensibly with window size. The long-horizon drift doesn't reach the part of the data anything actually reads.

**First dtime sweep on real odometry:** dtime=1 (78.4% vs. 76.7% zero-odometry — a real, confirmed gain) and dtime=5 (79.5% vs. 80.7% zero-odometry — a real, confirmed *regression*), both train≈test. dtime=10 in progress when the next issue below was found.

**Bigger issue found, mid-sweep, prompted by "1m/172s doesn't make physical sense" pushback that turned out to be right for a different reason than first suspected.** `frog_11-36_12-43_train_val.h5`'s real timestamps span ~25.6 hours, not one continuous session — it bundles two of the paper's own six recorded sessions (11:36 and 12:43 starts, per Table 1; the filename says as much) with a **~24.6-hour gap** between them, plus 61 much smaller (0.1-90s) within-session recording pauses. `integrate_trajectory()` was treating the whole file as one uninterrupted 40Hz stream. Checked directly rather than assumed: **31 of the 62 real gaps produced a small, plausible-*looking* ICP delta and slipped straight past the magnitude-based safety clamp** — including the 24.6-hour boundary itself, which came out looking like an innocuous "0.087m, 3.74°" step. The clamp only catches results that look wrong; it can't tell a genuinely small motion apart from ICP coincidentally finding a plausible-looking match between two unrelated scenes.

Fixed at two levels:
1. `integrate_trajectory()` now takes real `timestamps` and unconditionally zeros any transition exceeding `max_dt` (default 0.1s) *before* even asking ICP for an estimate — decided by ground-truth timing, not by whether the output happens to look plausible. (`tests/test_odometry_estimation.py`, 3 new tests including one that constructs a case where ICP *would* return a fine-looking wrong delta, confirming the timestamp check pre-empts it.)
2. **More fundamental, and not limited to odometry**: `FROG_Dataset` treated one `.h5` file as one *sequence*, so `get_scan()`'s temporal window had no way to know about a session boundary — meaning it was pulling raw-scan history across the ~24.6-hour gap into training windows for annotated frames shortly after it, independent of odometry entirely. This affected every FROG dtime experiment run this session, not just the pseudo-odometry ones (though the fraction of frames directly touched is small, ~2% at most, concentrated at the one boundary). Fixed by splitting `_load_h5()` into multiple *sessions* at any gap > 300s (a threshold that cleanly isolates only the one genuine ~24.6h boundary, leaving the 61 sub-90s pauses as ordinary in-sequence frames), mirroring DROW's own multi-sequence structure so `get_scan()`'s existing clamp-at-sequence-start logic protects session boundaries the same way it already protects the very start of a recording. Verified with 6 new tests against a synthetic h5 file (`tests/test_frog_session_splitting.py`): correct split count, re-based `det_id` per session, no data lost, and `get_scan()` confirmed *not* reaching across the boundary.

**Status now:** the pseudo-odometry file and the dtime=1/5 real-odometry comparisons above all predate this fix and should be regenerated/rechecked before being trusted further. Given the small directly-affected fraction, the broad zero-odometry dtime-sweep conclusion (item 1's update, dtime=5 as a peak) is probably still substantially right, but the finer 1-2pp real-vs-zero-odometry deltas this item has been chasing are exactly the scale this bug could plausibly explain — not confirmed to be the sole cause, but not ruled out either. Next: regenerate pseudo-odometry with both fixes in place, then redo the dtime=1/5/10 comparison on clean data before drawing conclusions.

**Bigger discovery: this whole ICP-pseudo-odometry effort was solving a problem the dataset doesn't actually have.** The FROG paper's own dataset page (`https://robotics.upo.es/datasets/frog/laser2d_people/data/`) hosts real, official per-session odometry as `frog_<HH-MM>_odom.npz` files (`ts` + `data` arrays, X/Y/heading, ~10Hz — sparser than scan rate, needs `np.interp` alignment) for all six recording sessions. `FROG_Dataset.download()` only ever fetched the `.h5` scan files, never these — so `_load_or_fake_odom()` was silently falling back to zero odometry on every FROG run to date, which is the actual root cause that motivated building an ICP estimator in the first place. Cross-checked, not just assumed: the real odometry files' own session-boundary timestamp gap (88,658s) matches the gap independently found in scan timestamps (88,655s) to within 3 seconds.

Fixed end-to-end:
- `FROG_Dataset.download()` (`library/follow_the_drow/datasets/frog_dataset.py`) now also fetches each session's matching `_odom.npz` after its `.h5` file, best-effort (wrapped in try/except — falls back to estimated/zero odometry with a warning if a fetch fails, never blocks the h5 download itself).
- `_load_h5()` parses session tokens (`\d{2}-\d{2}`) out of the h5 filename via a new `_session_odom_filenames()` helper, and prefers a matching real per-session file over the shared pseudo-odometry sidecar when present.
- New tests (`tests/test_frog_session_splitting.py`, `TestSessionOdomFilenames` + `TestRealOdometryPreference`, 5 tests) cover filename parsing and per-session file preference/fallback with synthetic data; full suite at 63 passing.
- Verified against the real files too: after placing `frog_11-36_odom.npz`/`frog_12-43_odom.npz` (downloaded manually, since only `train_val.h5`'s two sessions were needed locally), loading `FROG_Dataset(split='train')` shows x/y/theta ranges exactly matching the real files' own reported ranges (seq0: x∈[0,147.03] y∈[-102.68,9.43]; seq1: x∈[0,141.68] y∈[-74.03,50.42]).
- Also fixed, found along the way: `_download_file()`'s progress print used a Unicode arrow (`→`) that crashed with `UnicodeEncodeError` on Windows' default cp1252 console — silently broke real downloads (crashed *after* a 189MB file had already fully saved, right at the success message). Same class of bug as an earlier `--help`-text crash found this session; this one actually blocked functionality rather than being cosmetic.

**Consequence:** every real-odometry number in this item so far (dtime=1: 78.4%/78.4%, dtime=5: 79.6%/79.5%, dtime=10: val 75.5%) was trained on estimated ICP pseudo-odometry, a single-session dataset (pre session-splitting fix), or both — none of it reflects the actual official odometry now wired in. A from-scratch dtime=1/5/10 retrain sweep on the fixed pipeline (real odometry + correct session splitting) is needed before any real-vs-zero-odometry conclusion can be trusted; not yet scheduled as of this writing — deferred while the published-weights comparison (below) is worked on instead. `frog_16-41_odom.npz`/`frog_10-31_odom.npz`/`frog_14-57_odom.npz`/`frog_15-53_odom.npz` (the four sessions not in `train_val.h5`) haven't been fetched into the real dataset directory yet, only exercised transiently via a `download()` pipeline test.

**Published-weights cross-check, in parallel.** The same dataset page also hosts the paper's own trained checkpoints (PeTra ×2, LFE-Peaks/LFE-PPN, DROW3 T=1, DR-SPAAM T=1, DR-SPAAM T=5) — useful as *verified* (author-trained, not this-repo-retrained) reference points for the comparison table. Checked compatibility directly rather than assuming it: both DR-SPAAM checkpoints (`dr_spaam_1_on_frog.pth`, `dr_spaam_5_on_frog.pth`) load into this repo's existing `DrSpaamDetector` class with **zero missing/unexpected keys** (hyperparameters inferred from tensor shapes: `num_pts=56`, `pedestrian_only=True`). DROW3's checkpoint (`drow_on_frog.pth`) does **not** match this repo's existing `DrowDetector` class — it's the same conv-block/gate family as DR-SPAAM minus the `gate` submodule, a genuinely different parameterization — so evaluating it would need a new/adapted architecture class; flagged, not built yet (scope decision deferred). PeTra's checkpoints haven't been investigated — different task framing entirely, would need its own evaluation harness (already anticipated in `docs/RESEARCH.md`). ## 5. DROW-native accuracy ideas that avoid heavy (DR-SPAAM-style) preprocessing

**Status:** not started — idea list logged, queued after item 4.

**Framing constraint:** DR-SPAAM's *slowness* comes from running ~450 independent per-beam network passes over pre-made cutouts; its *accuracy* advantage plausibly comes from a separate thing — each crop being locally centered/scale-normalized before that pass. The two are separable. Every idea below keeps the single full-scan forward pass that makes our architecture faster than DR-SPAAM's — none of them reintroduce per-beam-independent inference.

1. **Beam-index shift augmentation** (cheapest, most surgical): randomly roll the whole scan and its labels by a random beam offset each training batch. Directly attacks the memorization mechanism identified earlier this session (the network can learn "beam 200 = the doorway in this specific room" because `raw_scan()`/`aligned_raw_scan()` preserve absolute beam position end-to-end) — zero architecture change, zero inference-time cost.
2. **Frame-to-frame difference channels**: now that `aligned_raw_scan()` correctly ego-motion-compensates, feed the current frame plus (T-1) delta channels (`frame[t] − frame[t-1]`, post-alignment) instead of T raw stacked frames — makes "temporal" explicitly about motion (static background → ~0) rather than repeated absolute position. Just elementwise subtraction of frames already computed.
3. **Local/relative range normalization**: subtract a small local-window mean/median from each beam once, before the single full-scan conv pass — the DR-SPAAM-inspired centering idea, stripped of DR-SPAAM's expensive per-beam architecture.
4. **Range/scale jitter augmentation**: randomly scale/offset a whole frame's range values during training, forcing invariance to absolute distance/room-depth rather than removing that information via a fixed deterministic transform. Complements #3.
5. **Frozen-backbone fine-tuning from FROG**: the earlier full fine-tune regressed *below* zero-shot transfer (14.6% vs. 26.7-28.0% wp-AUC) — plausibly catastrophic forgetting from unfreezing the whole network on DROW's tiny (17.6k-frame) training set. Freezing the early backbone (likely generic local-shape features) and only tuning later/output layers is a cheap training-flag change that could recover fine-tuning's promise without that failure mode.
6. **Class-imbalance check** (lower priority): verify whether the classification loss already weights the likely heavily-imbalanced person-vs-background beam classes; a class-balanced or focal loss is a standard, cheap lever if it doesn't.

**Recommended order:** #1 and #2 first — cheapest, most direct, and each targets a concrete, already-verified mechanism (absolute-position memorization; lack of an explicit motion signal) rather than a hypothesis still needing confirmation.
